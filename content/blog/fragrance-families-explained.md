Product pages love words like "fougère", "aromatic" and "amber woody". They're not just marketing. They describe broad scent families, and learning them makes shopping much easier.

## Fresh (citrus, aquatic, green)

Bright and clean. Citrus notes like bergamot, lemon and grapefruit; "aquatic" or "marine" accords that evoke sea air; and green notes like leaves or cut grass. These are the go-to for hot weather and daytime.

## Aromatic and fougère

"Aromatic" usually means herbs: lavender, rosemary, sage, mint. **Fougère** (French for "fern") is a classic men's structure built around lavender, coumarin (a sweet, hay-like note) and oakmoss. Many famous men's fragrances are modern fougères.

## Woody

Cedar, sandalwood, vetiver and patchouli. Woods often form the base of a fragrance, giving it depth and lasting power. A mostly woody scent tends to feel calm, mature and polished.

## Amber and spicy (often called "oriental")

Warm and rich: vanilla, tonka bean, resins, cinnamon, cardamom. These project warmth and sweetness and shine in cool weather and at night.

## How notes unfold

Fragrances are often described as a pyramid:

1. **Top notes:** what you smell first. They're bright and fade fastest.
2. **Heart notes:** the main character, appearing after the opening.
3. **Base notes:** the long-lasting foundation.

So a fragrance can open fresh and dry down warm. That's why it's worth wearing a sample for a few hours before deciding.
