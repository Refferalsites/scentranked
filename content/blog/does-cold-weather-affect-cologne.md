**Short answer:** Yes. Cold air slows evaporation, so fragrance projects less and can smell quieter in winter. That's why warmer, denser scents (vanilla, amber, spice, woods) and stronger concentrations tend to work better in cold weather.

## What changes in the cold

- Scent molecules evaporate more slowly, so less of the fragrance reaches the people around you.
- Light citrus and aquatic notes can seem to vanish quickly.
- Heavy layers of clothing trap the scent.

## How to adjust

1. **Switch to warmer scents** with sweet, spicy or woody bases.
2. **Consider an EDP or Parfum** for more presence.
3. **Spray on skin under your clothes,** chest and neck, so body heat lifts it.
