Walk down any fragrance aisle and you'll see the same scent sold three or four different ways: Eau de Toilette, Eau de Parfum, Parfum, sometimes "Intense" or "Elixir". The words describe **concentration**, meaning how much perfume oil is in the alcohol base. They don't tell you the quality.

## The rough ranges

- **Eau de Cologne (EDC):** the lightest. A quick, fresh splash.
- **Eau de Toilette (EDT):** light to moderate. Usually brighter and fresher.
- **Eau de Parfum (EDP):** more concentrated. Usually warmer and denser, and it tends to last longer.
- **Parfum / Extrait / Elixir:** the most concentrated. Often sits closer to the skin but lingers.

There's no legal standard for these percentages, so one brand's EDT can outlast another brand's EDP. Treat the labels as a guide, not a guarantee.

## Same name, different scent

Here's the part most people miss: flankers are often **reformulated, not just strengthened**. Le Male Le Parfum isn't simply Le Male EDT with more oil. It drops much of the minty freshness and leans into cardamom, iris and vanilla. Think of it as a sibling, not a stronger copy.

## Which should you buy?

1. **Hot climate, office, or daytime:** start with the EDT. It's usually fresher and harder to overdo.
2. **Cooler months, evenings, or you want it to last all day:** look at the EDP or Parfum.
3. **Already love a scent but wish it lasted longer?** Sample the stronger version first. It may smell noticeably different.

## A note on "longevity"

How long a fragrance lasts depends heavily on your skin, the weather, and how much you apply. Moisturized skin tends to hold scent better than dry skin. Spraying your clothes makes it last longer too, but test on a hidden spot first, as some fragrances can mark fabric.
